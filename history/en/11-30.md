## 2026 {#y2026}

On 12 November 1980, the Defense Security Command telephoned the heads of the country's news organisations on behalf of the military group then holding power in South Korea, known as the "new military" (신군부). Two days later, the newspaper and broadcasting associations adopted a resolution as instructed, announcing the forced merger and closure of media outlets that became known as the press consolidation (언론통폐합). According to an account by the National Human Rights Commission of Korea, 46 of the country's 64 media outlets were shut down or lost control of their management as a result, and each announced this in its own pages on 17 November. The privately owned Tongyang Broadcasting Company (TBC) and Dong-A Broadcasting System (DBS) went off the air on 30 November and were absorbed into the public broadcaster KBS, while the Christian Broadcasting System (CBS) was barred from broadcasting news and allowed only religious programmes. In the same account, after a female announcer cried while reading the last news bulletin, the new military ordered that no one cry on the farewell broadcasts, yet a singer and an actor still wept on TBC's farewell programme.

In 2010, the Truth and Reconciliation Commission, set up to investigate past state violence, found that the consolidation had violated freedom of expression and recommended that the state apologise. More than 1,000 journalists had been dismissed during the consolidation. When South Korea declared martial law on 3 December 2024, Decree No. 1 of the martial law command placed all press and publishing under the command's control and said that violators could be arrested, detained and searched without a warrant under the Martial Law Act (text as transcribed on Korean Wikisource). In 1980 Taiwan was under martial law, which was not lifted until 1987, and its Publication Act (出版法) was not repealed until 1999.
