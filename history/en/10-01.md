## 2026 {#y2026}

As of 1 October, anyone commenting on a website or app in China must first verify their real identity: the page can show only a nickname, but the platform must be able to match the account to a real person. The requirement comes from the Provisions on the Administration of Internet Comment Services, which took effect on 1 October 2017 and which the Cyberspace Administration of China framed as "real names in the back end, voluntary in the front end". A revised version that took effect in December 2022 replaced them and named mobile phone numbers and identity document numbers as means of verification. From July 2025, six agencies including the Ministry of Public Security introduced a state-issued "network number" and "network certificate", which users may choose to verify themselves to platforms with, after which platforms may not ask for their identity details in plain form. Those who do not opt in still verify with a phone number or similar. The measures also state that where laws require assistance, the state platform must provide the real identity behind a network number and its authentication logs.
