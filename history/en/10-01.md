## 2026 {#y2026}

On 1 October 2017, China's Provisions on the Administration of Internet Comment Services, issued by the Cyberspace Administration of China, took effect. Websites and apps offering comments had to verify users' real identities under the principle of "real names in the back end, voluntary in the front end". Users could post under a nickname, but the platform had to be able to match the account to a real identity, and unverified users could not comment. A revised version that took effect on 15 December 2022 replaced these rules and named mobile phone numbers, identity document numbers or unified social credit codes as the means of verification. From 15 July 2025, measures issued by six agencies including the Ministry of Public Security introduced a state-issued "network number" and "network certificate", which the measures describe as voluntary, with the state platform collecting only what verification requires. The age-verification debates of the past two years in many countries turn on the same question of how much a website needs to know about its users, and the EU's age-verification app takes a different approach (see [Privacy design and disputes around the EU age verification app](https://anoni.net/news/en/2026/09/eu-age-verification-blueprint/)).
