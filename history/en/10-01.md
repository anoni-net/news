## 2026 {#y2026}

On 1 October 2017, China's Provisions on the Administration of Internet Comment Services, issued by the Cyberspace Administration of China, took effect. Comment features on websites and apps had to verify users' identities under the principle of "real names in the back end, voluntary in the front end": users could post under a nickname, but the platform had to be able to match the account to a real identity, and unverified users could not comment. A revised version that took effect in December 2022 replaced these rules and named mobile phone numbers and identity document numbers as means of verification. From July 2025, six agencies including the Ministry of Public Security introduced a state-issued "network number" and "network certificate". Users may choose to verify themselves to platforms with them, in which case platforms may not ask for their identity details in plain form. Those who do not opt in still verify with a phone number or similar, and platforms must serve both groups equally. As of 1 October, the requirement to verify a real identity before commenting is unchanged, and the network number adds a way to do so without handing an ID number to every platform.
