## 2026 {#y2026}

In an assessment published in March 2023 by Shanghai's municipal consumer rights protection commission (republished by Sina Finance), all 10 commonly used apps tested had a way to turn off personalised recommendations (content picked from what you browse and tap), and only Xiaohongshu (RedNote) said that turning them off would not stop them entirely. China's Personal Information Protection Law took effect on 1 November 2021 and requires businesses that push information or marketing through automated decision-making (choosing content by a person's characteristics) to also offer an option not based on those characteristics or an easy way to refuse, and under the 2022 algorithmic recommendation provisions a provider must stop the related service immediately once a user switches recommendations off. State bodies handling personal information must also notify the people concerned under the 2021 law, unless other laws require confidentiality or notice would hinder the body in performing its statutory duties. The Personal Data Protection Act of Taiwan does not mention advertising or recommendations, and its Article 20 covers only marketing, with no requirement for a recommendation switch or an option not based on personal characteristics. Article 20(2) and (3) do require a business that uses personal data for marketing under that article to stop once you refuse and to offer a way to refuse the first time it markets to you, so readers in Taiwan can turn down marketing that uses their personal data (checked in October 2026).
