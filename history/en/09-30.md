## 2026 {#y2026}

On 30 September 2021, the DST Root CA X3 cross-sign that the free HTTPS certificate service Let's Encrypt had relied on since its early years expired. A phone trusts a site's HTTPS certificate only if it has the issuer's root certificate built in. Old phones that no longer receive system updates lack Let's Encrypt's own root, so sites using its certificates show warnings. Let's Encrypt's explainer named the iPhone 4 and the HTC Dream as examples, while older Android versions got three more years from a special cross-sign, which also expired on 30 September 2024. In 2023 Let's Encrypt advised users of Android 7.0 and earlier to switch to Firefox, which carries its own list of root certificates instead of relying on the system. In September 2025 Mozilla announced that Firefox 144 would require Android 8.0, leaving phones on Android 7 and earlier with version 143, which no longer receives updates. As of 1 October, people whose phones are stuck on Android 7 or earlier can no longer fall back on a browser that gets security updates, and a phone that can update its system is the longer-term fix.
