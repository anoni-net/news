## 2026 {#y2026}

On 27 November 1986, five Japanese Communist Party members of the Machida city assembly came to the Machida Telegraph and Telephone Office of Nippon Telegraph and Telephone (NTT). They said the home phone of the party's head of international affairs had noise on the line and might be tapped, and asked the office to investigate urgently. A National Police Agency official told the Judicial Affairs Committee of the House of Councillors in December that the telephone office had notified the Machida Police Station of the Tokyo Metropolitan Police Department that afternoon. According to an official of the Ministry of Posts and Telecommunications, relaying NTT's report, NTT examined the nearby junction box and found an extra lead-in wire connected inside it to that phone's line.

In 1989, the Supreme Court of Japan dismissed an appeal by the party's head of international affairs, who had sought to have officers of the Kanagawa Prefectural Police committed for trial, and adopted the lower courts' finding that the officers had acted in the course of their duties to obtain security intelligence on the Communist Party. In its decision, the court also wrote that the tapping violated the Telecommunications Business Act but did not amount to the crime of abuse of authority by a public official, because the officers had pretended throughout that the tapping was not done by police officers; in 1997, the Tokyo High Court held Kanagawa Prefecture and the national government liable for damages. Japan's Act on Wiretapping for Criminal Investigation (犯罪捜査のための通信傍受に関する法律), enacted in 1999, limits interception to the serious offences listed in its schedules and requires a warrant issued by a judge, and in Taiwan a surveillance warrant during an investigation is issued by a court. Figures from Taiwan's Judicial Yuan, which oversees the courts, show that the district courts approved 83.3% of prosecutors' applications for surveillance in 2023, and of the 1,775 cases in which notice to the people monitored was still being deferred at the end of that year, 616 had been deferred for more than a year. In Hong Kong, the 2024 annual report of the Commissioner on Interception of Communications and Surveillance records that law enforcement agencies made 1,054 written applications for interception that year, and panel judges appointed under the Interception of Communications and Surveillance Ordinance approved all of them.
