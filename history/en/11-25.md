## 2026 {#y2026}

When a House of Representatives investigating committee asked ten screenwriters and directors whether they were members of the Communist Party, they refused to answer, citing freedom of speech and association, and on 24 November 1947 the House voted to cite them for contempt of Congress. Forty-eight film company executives and lawyers met behind closed doors for two days at the Waldorf-Astoria Hotel in New York, and according to Deadline, four of them objected. One of the objectors wrote in a 1979 memoir that someone raised a California law barring employers from firing people for their political ideas, while a lawyer argued that the ten could be let go under the morals clause in their contracts for bringing disrepute on the industry. In the same account, the president of the Motion Picture Association of America threw down his hotel key and threatened to resign, and the decision to fire the ten was made without a vote. In their statement of 25 November, the executives announced that the ten would be discharged or suspended and pledged "We will not knowingly employ a Communist", and they acknowledged the policy's risks, writing "There is the danger of hurting innocent people" and "We will guard against this danger".

The Hollywood blacklist began with this statement and later grew to hundreds of people, and some screenwriters kept working under pseudonyms or by having friends put their names on scripts. Sections 1101 and 1102 of the California Labor Code prohibit employers from adopting rules that control employees' political activities and from threatening to fire them over political activity. Article 5 of Taiwan's Employment Service Act bars employers from discriminating against job applicants or employees for their beliefs (思想) or party affiliation (黨派). As of 22 November 2026, Article 3 of mainland China's Employment Promotion Law says workers may not face discrimination in employment because of ethnicity, race, sex, religious belief and so on (等). That list does not name political views or party affiliation.
