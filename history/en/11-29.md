## 2026 {#y2026}

On 29 November 1934, the Nationalist government promulgated the Martial Law Act (戒嚴法), and its text as amended in 1948 allowed the supreme commander of an area under martial law to open mail and telegrams. Taiwan became a martial law area on 20 May 1949 and stayed one until martial law was lifted on 15 July 1987. According to an entry in the National Human Rights Memory Bank (國家人權記憶庫), the Taiwan Garrison Command was formed in 1958 by merging four units, including the Taiwan Provincial Security Command. Its Special Inspection Department, which inspected mail and telegrams, grew out of a department of the same name in the Security Command. The entry says the department had 34 inspection teams across Taiwan.

After martial law ended, the statute on spot checks of mail and telegrams (戡亂時期郵電抽查條例) stayed in force for about seven and a half years, until it was abolished in 1995. It let military commands inspect mail and telegrams after the military and local administrative authorities obtained approval from the Executive Yuan (the cabinet). Taiwan's Communication Security and Surveillance Act of 1999 originally let prosecutors issue surveillance warrants during investigations, and in 2007 the Council of Grand Justices held that this did not conform to the freedom of confidential communication protected by the Constitution. As of 21 November 2026, a warrant during an investigation must be requested by a prosecutor and issued by a court. In mainland China, a separate Martial Law (戒严法) adopted in 1996 lets the authorities enforcing martial law decide, while it is in effect, to impose controls on communications, post and telecommunications in the affected areas (Article 13).
