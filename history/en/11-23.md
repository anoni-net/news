## 2026 {#y2026}

According to a 2019 article by the Committee to Protect Journalists (CPJ), in 2009 the governor of Maguindanao province in the southern Philippines came from a local political clan with its own private militia. On 23 November, a convoy accompanied by journalists set out to file candidacy papers for a challenger to the governor. Those killed had agreed to travel together because they believed an attack on so many people was unlikely. The convoy was ambushed on an open road, and the clan's militia, with the support of local police, killed 58 people, 32 of them journalists and media workers. In CPJ's records up to 2019, it was the deadliest single event for the press, and the deadliest before it, according to CPJ's 2010 annual report, was the killing of 11 employees of a television station in Iraq in 2006.

In the same article, CPJ reported that a court convicted two masterminds and 26 accomplices of murder ten years after the attack, with at least 80 suspects still at large at the time of the verdict. As of 21 November 2026, Reporters Without Borders (RSF) had counted three journalists killed in the Philippines since 1 January 2026, a tally shown on its country page. Across Asia, CPJ counted 110 journalists in prison on 1 December 2025, a third of the world's total, including 50 in China (seven of them in Hong Kong) and at least 16 in Vietnam. Working journalists and media workers facing an emergency because of their reporting can apply to CPJ for short-term emergency support, and CPJ gives priority to emergencies under its stated policy. Other readers do not need to take any action.
