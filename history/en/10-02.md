## 2026 {#y2026}

Data with names removed can still point back to you. On 2 October 2006, Netflix launched a contest to improve its recommendation algorithm and released a large set of member movie ratings, with rules stating that customer IDs had been replaced by randomly assigned ones. Two weeks later, researchers matched the data against public ratings on IMDb, identified the records of known users and, as their paper put it, uncovered their apparent political preferences and other potentially sensitive information. In Asia, Taiwan's Constitutional Court ruled in August 2022 that national health insurance data could not be used for research without a way for people to ask for it to stop. It found a violation of the right to informational privacy and gave lawmakers three years to fix it. Taiwan's Ministry of Health and Welfare said the National Health Insurance Data Management Act, passed in December 2025, sets out how people can ask for their data to stop being used, and the exceptions. Taiwan residents who want out can apply to the National Health Insurance Administration.
