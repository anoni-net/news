## 2026 {#y2026}

Facebook posts you see can be restricted from view in Hong Kong at the request of the authorities there under local law (restrictions rose from 402 in 2019 to 2,181 in 2023, according to public data from Meta cited by Radio Free Asia in October 2024). On 22 November 2016, The New York Times, citing three current and former Facebook employees who asked for anonymity, reported that the company had developed software to keep posts out of news feeds in specific geographic areas to help it enter China, where Facebook was blocked, and that the software had so far gone unused. According to the report, Facebook did not intend to suppress posts itself but would offer the software to a third-party partner, most likely a Chinese company, which would have full control over whether posts appeared, a step beyond the company's earlier practice in other countries of blocking content at government request after it had been posted. The report also said there was no indication that Facebook had offered the software to the authorities in China, and the company's statements to TechCrunch and Fortune said it was interested in China and had not decided on its approach, without addressing the software itself. Readers in Taiwan do not need to change their Facebook settings because of this report, while in mainland China, the market the report said the software was built for, facebook.com does not open from networks inside the country (according to the third-party measurement site GreatFire, the last 61 conclusive tests all failed; we checked on 15 November 2026).
