## 2026 {#y2026}

If you have family or friends in Iran, their internet connection had largely returned three months after the shutdown that began at the end of February 2026, though measurements showed they still faced heavy filtering, according to a 28 May post by NetBlocks, which monitors internet connectivity, as carried by Iran Focus (we checked on 14 November 2026). NetBlocks recorded on 17 November 2019 that the near-total national shutdown Iran had begun on the 16th had lasted 24 hours, with national connectivity at 5% of ordinary levels. According to an article by OONI (Open Observatory of Network Interference), a project that measures internet censorship, the shutdown began after the government abruptly announced a fuel price increase and protests broke out in multiple cities, and most Iranians could not reach the global internet and were left with websites and services hosted inside the country. Shutdowns like this remain common in Asia: according to a 31 March 2026 press release from the digital rights group Access Now, 195 of at least 313 shutdowns recorded worldwide in 2025 took place in 11 Asia Pacific countries, including at least 95 in Myanmar, and 65 in India. If you have weighed the risk described in OONI's FAQ, which depends on who monitors your network and which country you are in (the app is not a privacy tool, and a government, internet provider or employer monitoring your connection may see that you are running it), we, a community based in Taiwan, suggest using its measurement app, OONI Probe, to test whether websites and messaging apps such as WhatsApp and Telegram are blocked on your own network, and looking up measurements from Iran and elsewhere on OONI Explorer, the site that publishes OONI's open data.
