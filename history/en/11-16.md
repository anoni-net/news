## 2026 {#y2026}

If you travel to mainland China from elsewhere in Asia, for work or on holiday, twitter.com (the domain Twitter used before it was renamed X) still will not open on your phone. GreatFire, which has long measured the Great Firewall (the censorship system that blocks foreign websites from inside China), reports that 98% of its last 59 conclusive tests failed; it measures only twitter.com, and when we checked on 14 November 2026 the most recent test was on 10 November. On 16 November 2009 the US president at the time held a town hall with university students in Shanghai, which was not carried live on national television but was shown on local Shanghai TV and online, and the US ambassador to China read out a question sent in to the US Embassy website asking whether the president knew of the firewall in "a country with 350 million Internet users and 60 million bloggers" and whether people there should be able to use Twitter freely. The White House transcript records him answering that he had never used Twitter, that he believed in openness in the flow of information and was "a big supporter of non-censorship", and that "different countries have different traditions", and The Christian Science Monitor (CSMonitor) reported that his response was "faithfully transcribed onto the website of the official state news agency, Xinhua" and posted prominently on all four major Chinese web portals. If you are heading to mainland China, you can look up the addresses you use most on GreatFire before you leave (a suggestion from us, not from GreatFire).
