## 2026 {#y2026}

If your personal details are posted online in Hong Kong, you can turn to the Office of the Privacy Commissioner for Personal Data, which can require platforms to remove them, including social media platforms based overseas. On 8 October 2021, the Personal Data (Privacy) (Amendment) Ordinance 2021 was gazetted and took effect; the commissioner's announcement said it aims to combat doxxing acts that intrude on personal data privacy, and it made doxxing a criminal offence and gave the office powers of criminal investigation and to issue cessation notices. Figures the office published in February 2026 show that by the end of 2025 it had handled 3,634 doxxing cases and issued 2,104 cessation notices to 57 online platforms, most of them served on overseas operators, with an overall compliance rate above 96%. Before the law passed, the Asia Internet Coalition, whose members include Apple, Facebook and Google, wrote in July 2021 that the possibility of prosecuting local staff would create uncertainty for businesses and that the only way for tech companies to avoid such sanctions would be to refrain from investing and offering their services in Hong Kong, as quoted by The Register.
