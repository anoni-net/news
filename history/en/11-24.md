## 2026 {#y2026}

South Korea's president died in October 1979, and from 27 October martial law was in force across the country except Jeju Island. According to a weekly magazine's account, getting permission for a rally under martial law was effectively impossible, so dissidents disguised one as a wedding at the YWCA in Myeong-dong, Seoul. In the same article, the groom's younger sister recalled that the police detective assigned to watch him came to the house several times beforehand to ask "Is your son really getting married?", and each time their mother answered "He is." On the evening of 24 November, the moment the groom walked in, people at the venue handed out a statement opposing the indirect election of the president by the National Conference for Unification under the Yushin Constitution. Security forces then burst in, and more than 140 people were taken away in all.

According to the same magazine article, the torture of those detained came out at trial, and 14 of them were held in custody and given prison terms. According to Newsis, a retrial at the Seoul High Court in November 2019 acquitted the dissidents who had been sentenced to prison, because the martial-law proclamation used to convict them was unconstitutional and void. South Korea's current Assembly and Demonstration Act requires organisers of outdoor assemblies to notify the police in advance, and on 26 February 2026 the Constitutional Court held that its provision punishing every failure to notify was incompatible with the Constitution, and the law has yet to be amended. As of 21 November 2026, the Taipei City Government's law database lists Taiwan's Assembly and Parade Act as requiring no application for an ordinary indoor assembly, and requiring organisers of an ordinary outdoor assembly or parade to apply for permission six days ahead. The full text of mainland China's Law on Assemblies, Processions and Demonstrations, reposted on 12371.cn, requires organisers of assemblies, processions and demonstrations in open-air public places to apply to the local public security bureau and obtain permission.
