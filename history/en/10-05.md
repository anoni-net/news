## 2026 {#y2026}

If you get login codes by text message, those messages are not encrypted on telecom networks, and the mobile communications guidance from the US Cybersecurity and Infrastructure Security Agency (CISA) says anyone with access to a carrier's network can read them. On 5 October 2024, The Wall Street Journal reported that US broadband providers had been breached, and that the attack targeted the systems carriers use to handle court-ordered wiretaps. In a joint statement in November, the FBI and CISA said actors linked to the Chinese government had stolen call records and copied information subject to law enforcement requests under court orders, and a 2025 advisory co-sealed by agencies including Japan's National Police Agency said the activity has been observed in the United States, Australia, Canada, New Zealand, the United Kingdom and elsewhere. Canada's Bill C-22, in our story the same day, would require providers to build exactly this kind of capability to assist law enforcement (see [Canada's Bill C-22, lawful access and encryption](https://anoni.net/news/en/2026/10/canada-c22-lawful-access/)). CISA advises using end-to-end encrypted messaging apps and moving account verification away from text messages to codes from an authenticator app.
