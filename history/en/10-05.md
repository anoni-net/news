## 2026 {#y2026}

Ordinary text messages on your phone are not end-to-end encrypted, and Google's help page for Google Messages says end-to-end encryption is not available for SMS or MMS, only for RCS chats. On 5 October 1994, the US House of Representatives passed the Communications Assistance for Law Enforcement Act (CALEA), which requires telecom carriers to make their equipment capable of assisting law enforcement wiretaps; it was signed into law on 25 October. The law also says a carrier is not responsible for decrypting communications that a subscriber encrypted, unless the carrier provided the encryption and holds the information needed to decrypt it. Canada's Bill C-22, in our story the same day, would likewise require providers to build capabilities to assist law enforcement (see [Canada's Bill C-22, lawful access and encryption](https://anoni.net/news/en/2026/10/canada-c22-lawful-access/)). If you use Google Messages, a lock on the send button means the conversation is end-to-end encrypted.
