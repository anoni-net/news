## 2026 {#y2026}

As of 1 October, when you use Chrome to visit a site that supports it, the site name sent at the start of the connection is encrypted, so network operators cannot learn from that part which site you are visiting. Safari on iPhone does not support it yet: in August 2026 a developer reported on Apple's developer forums that Safari still sends the site name in plain text. The technology is called Encrypted Client Hello (ECH), and on 29 September 2023 Cloudflare announced it was available on all of its plans. Both the site and the browser need to support it; Firefox does too, and ECH became a formal internet standard in March 2026. The padlock in the address bar only means the page content is encrypted, since the site name and your DNS lookups have to be encrypted separately, and Cloudflare's [Browser Check](https://www.cloudflare.com/ssl/encrypted-sni/) page shows which of these your browser covers. China blocked an earlier version of ECH in July 2020, according to researchers from Geneva, GFW Report and iYouPort. Russia's telecom regulator began blocking these connections in November 2024, and Risky Biz News quoted it as saying the decision came after Cloudflare turned ECH on by default in October.
