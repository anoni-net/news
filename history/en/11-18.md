## 2026 {#y2026}

Apps such as Facebook on your phone can stop connecting when a government orders internet providers to block them; a report by OONI (an open project that measures internet censorship around the world) and a local group in Bangladesh suggests that Facebook was also blocked on the country's major mobile networks during the student protests of July 2024. On 18 November 2015, the Bangladesh Telecommunication Regulatory Commission (BTRC) ordered internet providers to block messaging and social media services, and the local news site bdnews24 quoted commission officials as listing Facebook, WhatsApp, Viber and others (reports differed on how many services the order covered). The state minister for posts and telecommunications announced that the services had been blocked for security reasons, according to bdnews24, while Global Voices cited the government as saying the ban stemmed from fears of unrest after the Supreme Court upheld the death sentences of two political figures convicted of crimes committed during the 1971 war of independence. According to Global Voices, Facebook was unblocked on 10 December, after the government had met Facebook representatives, and on 14 December the state minister announced that the remaining social media and messaging services were being unblocked as well.
