Law enforcement claims tying Pixel phones and GrapheneOS to crime, and the disputes around them

Security & Scams

Aftermath of the eMudhra weak private keys, and macOS command-line tools

After the private keys of eight intermediate certificates from the Indian certificate authority eMudhra were computed, Apple, Microsoft and Chrome have all blocked or removed the related certificates, and ordinary users' browsers, phones and computers need no extra setup. The git, curl and other command-line tools built into macOS may still accept these certificates, and as of 2 November we had found no fix from Apple. eMudhra has announced it is leaving the public TLS certificate business, and as of 1 November had not filed a formal incident report on the incident ticket.

Based on 10 sources, including Apple, Microsoft 365 Message Center Archive, Mozilla Bugzilla

Norway's fine on Xandr over Grindr user data in real-time bidding

On 12 October, Norway's data protection authority announced a fine of NOK 92 million (about €8.5 million) on Xandr, an advertising technology company owned by Microsoft, for processing data on Grindr users in Norway through real-time bidding from 2018 to 2020 without a legal basis. Content takedown disputes in Indonesia and a civil society coalition weighing legal action

In October, the Indonesian digital rights group SAFEnet reviewed two years of digital controls under the current government and recorded at least 73 pieces of content taken down between April and September 2026, most of them critical of the government. The effectiveness of Iran's internet filtering and the government's countermeasures against Starlink

According to an October report by Filterwatch, a group that monitors Iran's internet, Iran's government fears its filtering is failing against satellite internet and has turned to signal jamming, equipment confiscation and diplomatic pressure. The secretary of the Supreme Council of Cyberspace claims Iran can already disable Starlink terminals, while the CEO of the Telecommunication Infrastructure Company, who is also a deputy minister of communications, said the ministry's official position is that current filtering policy has not been effective. Under a 2025 law, individuals who use unlicensed satellite internet equipment face imprisonment.

Tracking & Personal Data

Dispute over the UK Home Office's use of generative AI to summarise asylum interviews

In October, the UK digital rights group Open Rights Group published an investigation into two generative AI tools the Home Office uses in asylum cases, one of which summarises interview transcripts. Home Office subject matter experts checked 203 summaries and judged 22% unusable in the first round. The minister responsible for asylum wrote in an August letter that applicants are not specifically told AI was involved in handling their claims. According to the Home Office, the tools only assist and do not replace caseworkers' decisions.
