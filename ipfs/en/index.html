Censorship & Access Control

CircumVolve, a study using large language models to search for censorship evasion strategies

Researchers had a large language model repeatedly rewrite circumvention programs and, testing on networks in China, Iran, Pakistan and Kazakhstan, found 47 strategies that get past censorship equipment, though a strategy that works in one country does not necessarily work in another. The strategies reach users only after tool developers build them into their tools, and ordinary readers do not need to change any settings.

Based on 5 sources from GFW Report, GitHub

Security & Scams

The breach of Japanese marketing platform KARTE and member data from hundreds of companies

KARTE, a marketing platform run by the Japanese company PLAID, Inc., was broken into by an outside attacker on 13 November. PLAID's investigation findings of 20 November list member data imported by hundreds of companies, and records of visitors' behaviour on those companies' websites, as leaked or possibly leaked, including data from companies whose contracts had ended and whose data should already have been deleted. Ordinary visitors may not know that a website uses KARTE. Customers and members of these companies, and users of their websites and apps, can watch for notices from the companies.

Based on 13 sources, including PLAID, MUFG Bank, DHC

Tracking & Personal Data

Coding agent and developer tool records in Windows 11 diagnostic data

Testing on a Windows 11 virtual machine, a researcher found that a system component records which coding agents and which kinds of developer tools have been run, and that the record is classed as optional diagnostic data and placed in the upload queue. Optional diagnostic data can be turned off in Settings. Microsoft's list of required diagnostic data separately includes a cohort event that reports whether a user is a developer, and Microsoft has not explained how it relates to these records.

Based on 7 sources from xusheng.dev, Microsoft, Computerworld

UK secrecy over the Apple technical capability notice and the Investigatory Powers Tribunal ruling

The Investigatory Powers Tribunal ruled on 19 November that the UK government had not shown that publicly confirming it gave Apple a technical capability notice would damage national security. Whether the notice is lawful has not been heard and its content has not been published. The parties indicate they are likely to agree arrangements aimed at a substantive hearing in the first half of 2027, and according to Apple's support page, users outside the UK could still turn on iCloud Advanced Data Protection as of 21 November.

Security & Scams

The Bureau of Consular Affairs website breach and 72,702 leaked passport photo files

Taiwan's Bureau of Consular Affairs, part of the Ministry of Foreign Affairs, found on 4 November that some of its websites had been broken into. Some passport photos uploaded between 3 September and 2 November through online appointments, travel agency bulk applications and conditional online passport renewal were leaked, 72,702 files in all. Applications made over the counter or through the one-stop service at household registration offices are not included. According to the bureau's announcement and Q&A, passports remain valid, and people confirmed to be within the scope can get a free replacement until 14 May 2027 (checked on 15 November). Some letters of authorisation verified by Taiwan's representative office in New York and some ABTC application data were also leaked, and ABTC applicants can ask to change their photo until 31 December.

Based on 16 sources, including 外交部領事事務局, iThome, 風傳媒

Column

Tighter limits on outside contributions to open source projects in the AI era

curl ended its bug bounty, and GitHub now lets maintainers limit who can open pull requests and issues; in 2026 open source projects are narrowing the ways outsiders can contribute. The author argues that this addresses the volume problem created by floods of low-quality submissions, but the trust problem remains. The malicious releases in both the xz backdoor and the Shai-Hulud worm were published from developer accounts that were already trusted, and what is missing is review capacity and permission management.

Based on 23 sources, including curl author's blog, curl, GitHub

Phones & Apps

Phrases excluded from iPhone keyboard suggestions and swipe typing

An independent researcher recovered a phrase list Apple has not disclosed from the keyboard data in iOS 27.0, with 4,513 English entries and lists for 27 other languages and scripts. Listed phrases do not appear in predictive text and most cannot be swipe-typed, while typing letter by letter is unaffected. Apple's reply to Reuters said the keyboard is set up to avoid suggestions that might be inappropriate or offensive, and as of 15 November its support page offers no setting to turn the list off.

Based on 9 sources, including Apple, Reuters (via CP24), Swipeblocked

The TLS Middlebox experiment in OONI Probe and a survey on data collection

On 10 November OONI announced a TLS Middlebox experiment in OONI Probe for locating censorship equipment, and at announcement the apps supported only its default mode. An advanced mode, which could let others estimate the city a user is in, is available only in the command-line version and only to users who opt in. The netiCRM provider's server breach and a client's copied donation database

NETivism, the Taiwanese provider of netiCRM, a donation and membership management system for non-profits, had an internal management server broken into on 9 November. Automatic content recognition on smart TVs and manufacturers' answers to 17 questions

The Verge put 17 yes-or-no questions to 11 TV and streaming device makers, and 7 answered. Samsung, LG and Amazon told The Verge that automatic content recognition (ACR) is off by default, and Roku said its equivalent feature is off by default in the US.
