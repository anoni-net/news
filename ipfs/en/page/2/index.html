Security & Scams

Weak private keys in eight eMudhra intermediate certificates

Eight intermediate certificates from the Indian certificate authority eMudhra had private keys smaller than 256, which anyone could compute at once and use to issue HTTPS certificates for other people's websites. After a researcher reported it on 17 October, Chrome and Firefox blocked them automatically within hours, with nothing for ordinary browser users to set up. Programs that do not go through a browser carry their own trust lists, and Node.js, Python's certifi, Go, Java and Linux distributions have been shipping updates since 19 October (not all complete as of 22 October), so people who write their own code or run servers need to update each one.

Based on 14 sources, including Mozilla dev-security-policy, Mozilla Bugzilla, Google

Security & Scams

Implanted chips in Ledger hardware wallets

On 15 October Ledger confirmed that a criminal group had fitted genuine hardware wallets with parts that capture the recovery phrase during setup, then put them on the market through resellers in Southeast Asia. As of 13 October, the thefts Ledger had confirmed were all tied to devices sold by authorised reseller CryptoBilis, and reports of implants from other channels were unconfirmed. Ledger's Genuine Check cannot detect these implants, and Ledger advises anyone who bought and set up a device from CryptoBilis to move their assets to a new device and a new recovery phrase.

Based on 20 sources, including Ledger, Ledger Support, Ledger Academy

The passkey attestation debate

On 10 October a commentary argued against using passkeys because sites can use attestation to restrict which devices and software people may use, and on 14 October an author published a response. Both agree that passkeys are, in most cases, bound to a domain and resist phishing; they disagree on whether attestation will become a restriction, and on whether to keep using passkeys or switch to something else.

Based on 23 sources, including Non-Functional, scotthelme.co.uk, W3C

Surveillance & Spyware

Zero-click spyware delivered through targeted ads

Citizen Lab published a report on 15 October linking, with high confidence, attacks on at least 45 Al Jazeera devices between 2024 and 2025 to targeted ads from the Israeli ad firm Discover-Tech and spyware from Candiru. Al Jazeera is the only confirmed target, and the exploited vulnerabilities are not described. The advice for general readers is to block ads and limit advertising IDs, which according to the report lowers only part of the risk.
