Automatic content recognition on smart TVs and manufacturers' answers to 17 questions

The Verge put 17 yes-or-no questions to 11 TV and streaming device makers, and 7 answered. Samsung, LG and Amazon told The Verge that automatic content recognition (ACR) is off by default, and Roku said its equivalent feature is off by default in the US. In Northeastern University measurements reported by The Verge, Samsung, Roku and Vizio TVs still sent traffic consistent with ACR after opt-out, though the traffic was encrypted and its contents could not be confirmed. Samsung's Taiwan privacy policy says the service can be disabled in settings (checked on 14 November).

Based on 12 sources, including The Verge, arXiv, Samsung

Censorship & Access Control

Monitoring figures on social media takedowns in Indonesia and the communications ministry's response

On 12 November, the Indonesian groups SAFEnet and Remotivi released monitoring results recording 127 pieces of social media content restricted or removed as of 8 November, up from 73 in their previous release on 8 September. The two groups attribute 94 of them to requests from the Ministry of Communication and Digital Affairs. On 13 November a ministry official said it was willing to open its records for comparison, and that it is preparing a public notice mechanism to be used before access is cut off, with no date announced as of 14 November. Readers outside Indonesia are not directly affected.

Based on 10 sources, including SAFEnet, Kompas.com, CNN Indonesia

Encryption & Messaging

Machine-checked proofs for Signal's one-to-one chats and a US grand jury subpoena

Google DeepMind used AI agents to prove encryption properties of libsignal, the Signal Protocol implementation, for one-to-one chats. Group chats, the servers and key generation are outside the scope, and as of 14 November we could find no reproduction by other researchers. Signal published an October grand jury subpoena in which only one of four phone numbers had an account, and what it handed over was the account creation time and the last connection date. Users do not need to change any settings.

Based on 12 sources, including Google DeepMind, Signal, Ars Technica

New York City's surveillance pricing ban bill and the FTC's draft policy statement on personalized pricing

The New York City Council passed a bill on 12 November that, with some exceptions, bars businesses from using algorithms and personal data to charge different customers different prices for the same product. As of 14 November the bill was awaiting the mayor's signature, and it takes effect one year after becoming law. A draft policy statement the US Federal Trade Commission (FTC) proposed in August would instead require businesses to disclose personalized pricing in certain circumstances. Readers outside New York City are not directly affected by the city bill.

Security & Scams

The eMudhra incident report, macOS fixes and the deadline for the G1 root

The weak private key incident at the Indian certificate authority eMudhra had three developments in November. On 9 November Apple released macOS 27.2.1, 26.7.3 and 15.8.3, fixing git, curl and other built-in command-line tools. People who use these tools on a Mac should update, and everyone else needs no extra setup. On 10 November eMudhra filed an incident report covering the eight RSA intermediate certificates it separately revoked on 7 November. According to the source code we read on 14 November, Chrome and Mozilla no longer trust certificates chaining to emSign Root CA - G1 that are issued after 16 November UTC, and Microsoft has scheduled the removal of trust in G1 from Edge and WebView2 on the same day.

Based on 13 sources, including Mozilla Bugzilla, Apple, Microsoft 365 Message Center Archive

Webloc, Penlink's ad-based location surveillance system, and its customers in more countries

According to Citizen Lab's update of 12 November relaying a Bloomberg investigation, security agencies in more countries, including the UK, Germany and Singapore, have acquired Webloc, which tracks phone locations using app and advertising data. The confirmed customers in Citizen Lab's April report were in the United States, El Salvador and Hungary, and the update adds that Austria's Ministry of the Interior has been confirmed as a customer (checked on 14 November). Phone settings for tracking and location permissions can reduce what apps send out, but the report says they cannot reliably prevent it.

Tracking & Personal Data

Scope and opt-out of model training on Gemini personal account chats

Keep Activity is on by default for Gemini personal accounts held by people 18 or over, and while it is on Google uses activity to train generative AI models, with a subset of chats going to human reviewers (per Google's help pages, checked on 8 November). With the setting off (feedback excepted) or in a Temporary Chat, chats are not used for training and are kept for 72 hours, while human-reviewed chats are kept for up to three years. The help pages do not distinguish between paid and free plans.

Based on 16 sources, including Google, Hacker News, Personal Data Protection Commission, Singapore
