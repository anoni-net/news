Facial recognition and mall cameras in Hong Kong's SmartView programme

Security & Scams

OpenSSH login bypass with long passwords on musl systems

OpenSSH released 10.6p2 on 5 November, fixing a flaw that let an overly long password log in to accounts with no password set. Only sshd on Linux built with musl libc and not using PAM to check passwords is in scope, Alpine Linux being one example. According to the OpenSSH maintainer and HKCERT, glibc systems such as Debian, Ubuntu and Red Hat are not in scope, though as of 8 November the Debian and Ubuntu trackers had not marked them unaffected. Anyone running sshd on Alpine should update or turn off password logins.

Based on 16 sources, including OpenSSH, Alpine Linux, oss-security

Senate committee review of Canada's Bill C-22 and the dispute over built-in weaknesses

Canada's Bill C-22 on lawful access passed second reading in the Senate on 22 October and went to the Standing Senate Committee on National Security, Defence and Veterans Affairs, and as of 7 November it had not reached third reading. A Citizen Lab researcher argues that AI-driven cyber threats have changed significantly since June and that communications systems should not be left with weaknesses. Security & Scams

Aftermath of the eMudhra weak private keys, and macOS command-line tools

After the private keys of eight intermediate certificates from the Indian certificate authority eMudhra were computed, Apple, Microsoft and Chrome have all blocked or removed the related certificates, and ordinary users' browsers, phones and computers need no extra setup. The git, curl and other command-line tools built into macOS may still accept these certificates, and as of 2 November we had found no fix from Apple. eMudhra has announced it is leaving the public TLS certificate business, and as of 1 November had not filed a formal incident report on the incident ticket.
