Payment and identity in SimpleX supporter badges

SimpleX Chat sells supporter badges that let buyers send larger files. By design, SimpleX cannot tell from a badge it is shown which purchase the badge came from, but card payments and badges grouped by expiry week still leave traces.

Based on 10 sources, including SimpleX Chat, GitHub, IETF By anoni.net community

Also in 正體中文, 简体中文

SimpleX's announcement says that in other messaging apps a paid feature is attached to the account, so the operator knows who paid and what they do with the feature. Since September SimpleX Chat has sold supporter badges, which raise the file size limit from 1 GB to 2 GB (5 GB with a Legend badge) and keep files on servers for longer (7 days with a Supporter badge, 21 days with a Legend badge). The SimpleX network has no user accounts, so a badge is an anonymous credential that proves the holder paid without revealing who they are. As of 21 November, version 7.1 of the SimpleX Chat app includes the feature.

According to SimpleX's technical white paper, a badge is a BBS signature (a digital signature scheme still being standardised), issued by a badge service that SimpleX operates and stored on the user's own device. Each time the app shows the badge to a contact or presents it to a server, it generates a new zero-knowledge proof from it. A zero-knowledge proof works like proving you are over 18 without handing over your whole ID card, a technique we also covered in our piece on the EU age verification app. Contacts and server operators therefore receive only the badge type and expiry date, and cannot link two presentations to each other or trace either one back to the purchase.

SimpleX's announcement says credentials are issued one month at a time, and all badges expire on the same day of the week. A holder is therefore hidden among every supporter whose badge expires that week. According to the white paper, at each monthly renewal the app requests the new credential from the badge service and shows the new badge to contacts on different days, so that others find it harder to pick out a holder by timing.

Perspective

Privacy Pass, developed by the Internet Engineering Task Force (IETF), takes a different approach: a user passes a check once in exchange for tokens, and can present them without revealing who they are. According to the white paper, these tokens are spent once presented and carry no expiry date or type. A badge has to be presented again and again, in every conversation and on every connection, so SimpleX chose BBS signatures, from which the app can keep generating new proofs.

The white paper says the badge service keeps the payment record of each purchase, and a card payment is linked to the purchase, as with any other service. Because badges expiring in the same week share one expiry date, server operators can group those supporters' connections together, and how many people a holder is hidden among depends on how many badges expire that week. A badge that has just been bought or redeemed from a code is shown right away, so for people who get a code in the badge service's own group, SimpleX can infer from when the new badge appears who redeemed it. When a Google Play subscription is moved to a new device with Restore purchases (the feature that moves the store's subscription to the current profile), SimpleX can link the two purchases, though not the profiles that hold them.

The Android version installed from Google Play takes payment inside the app, with a one-month plan and monthly or annual plans that renew automatically. The iOS version, versions installed from F-Droid or GitHub, and the desktop version open a purchase page in the browser, where paying by card, Bitcoin or Monero gives you a code to paste into the app's settings in exchange for a badge. The white paper says that people who want the payment itself to be private can pay with cryptocurrency instead. Badges bought on the web do not renew automatically and, as of 21 November, cannot be moved to a new device.

Mainland China and Taiwan are two examples. As of 21 November the China App Store had no listing for SimpleX Chat, and GreatFire (a project that measures blocking by the Great Firewall) found play.google.com blocked in mainland China as of 1 November. The Taiwan App Store did list the app. GreatFire also found simplex.chat, where the announcement sends badge buyers, blocked in mainland China in its latest test on 17 November, based on a single sample.

For those who pay by card, the payment record may also stay with the card issuer. A card issuer covered by mainland China's Anti-Money Laundering Law must keep customer identity data and transaction records for at least ten years (Article 34). In Taiwan, the Money Laundering Control Act requires covered institutions to keep customer identification records for at least five years after the relationship or an occasional transaction ends (Article 8).

A badge is attached to a single profile, and incognito profiles do not show it. Badges cannot be revoked, so if a device or backup is compromised, whoever obtains the badge data can use it until it expires. If several months were bought, the intruder can also request new credentials for the remaining months from the badge service.
