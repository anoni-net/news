Aftermath of the eMudhra weak private keys, and macOS command-line tools

After the private keys of eight intermediate certificates from the Indian certificate authority eMudhra were computed, Apple, Microsoft and Chrome have all blocked or removed the related certificates, and ordinary users' browsers, phones and computers need no extra setup. The git, curl and other command-line tools built into macOS may still accept these certificates, and as of 2 November we had found no fix from Apple. eMudhra has announced it is leaving the public TLS certificate business, and as of 1 November had not filed a formal incident report on the incident ticket.

Based on 10 sources, including Apple, Microsoft 365 Message Center Archive, Mozilla Bugzilla

Measurements of censorship and code security gaps in China's open-weight Qwen models

Dispute over the removal of the Bluetooth mesh messaging app Bitchat in India

Norway's fine on Xandr over Grindr user data in real-time bidding

On 12 October, Norway's data protection authority announced a fine of NOK 92 million (about €8.5 million) on Xandr, an advertising technology company owned by Microsoft, for processing data on Grindr users in Norway through real-time bidding from 2018 to 2020 without a legal basis. A Microsoft spokesperson told the press the conduct predates the acquisition and the company is reviewing the decision. Users can delete the advertising ID or refuse tracking in phone settings, and the official help pages do not say whether those settings cover the other data named in the decision.

Content takedown disputes in Indonesia and a civil society coalition weighing legal action

In October, the Indonesian digital rights group SAFEnet reviewed two years of digital controls under the current government and recorded at least 73 pieces of content taken down between April and September 2026, most of them critical of the government. SAFEnet and other groups are also gathering potential plaintiffs to assess legal action against the Ministry of Communication and Digital Affairs. The ministry denies asking platforms to restrict content about forest fires, and in September the Constitutional Court ruled that the government must give notice with reasons before cutting off access. Readers outside Indonesia are not directly affected.

The effectiveness of Iran's internet filtering and the government's countermeasures against Starlink

According to an October report by Filterwatch, a group that monitors Iran's internet, Iran's government fears its filtering is failing against satellite internet and has turned to signal jamming, equipment confiscation and diplomatic pressure. The secretary of the Supreme Council of Cyberspace claims Iran can already disable Starlink terminals, while the CEO of the Telecommunication Infrastructure Company, who is also a deputy minister of communications, said the ministry's official position is that current filtering policy has not been effective. Under a 2025 law, individuals who use unlicensed satellite internet equipment face imprisonment.

Based on 18 sources, including Filterwatch, Zoomit, Tehran Times

Dispute over the UK Home Office's use of generative AI to summarise asylum interviews

In October, the UK digital rights group Open Rights Group published an investigation into two generative AI tools the Home Office uses in asylum cases, one of which summarises interview transcripts. Home Office subject matter experts checked 203 summaries and judged 22% unusable in the first round. The minister responsible for asylum wrote in an August letter that applicants are not specifically told AI was involved in handling their claims. According to the Home Office, the tools only assist and do not replace caseworkers' decisions.

Based on 20 sources, including Open Rights Group, Home Office, Home Office (published by Open Rights Group)

The lawsuit to revoke Thailand's notification on urgent takedowns of online content

In July, Thailand's Ministry of Digital Economy and Society published a notification defining the online data that may be the subject of an urgent court petition for suspension or removal under the Computer Crime Act, including 14 categories contrary to public order or good morals. On 14 October, Thai Lawyers for Human Rights and iLaw asked the Central Administrative Court to revoke the definition of "urgent necessity" and the two categories on the monarchy and on hatred. Tracking & Personal Data

The European Data Protection Board's preliminary guidance on smart glasses

On 13 October the European Data Protection Board adopted a statement on smart glasses, noting that because they look much like ordinary glasses, people nearby may be filmed or recorded without knowing it. The statement recommends that manufacturers signal recording with reliable lights and sounds that are hard to switch off, and that recording should not be possible while the light is covered. The press release presents the statement as preliminary guidance, and the statement sets no new rules and names no manufacturer.

Based on 19 sources, including European Data Protection Board, netzpolitik.org, Privacy Guides

Security & Scams

SSH login interception through compromised Tor exit relays

In early October the settings of more than 300 Tor exit relays were altered so that SSH connections through them went to interception servers. According to The Hacker News, anyone who logged in to SSH over Tor with a password between 1 and 5 October after accepting an unfamiliar or changed host key should treat that password as stolen. The relays were gone from Tor's list of usable relays by 01:00 on 6 October Taipei time, and according to the person who found them, encrypted web traffic was passed on as usual.

Based on 10 sources, including Tor Project, tor-relays, kaba.cx

Disputed matching of protest footage by Baden-Württemberg police and Berlin's behaviour-recognition cameras

According to court testimony reported by netzpolitik.org, an officer at the State Criminal Police Office of Baden-Württemberg in Germany ran footage of a registered demonstration through a computer after the event and identified four participants who, to date, have no connection to the case. In October, Berlin police began testing cameras that analyse behaviour at high-crime locations, and the police law rules out automated facial recognition on that footage. The EU AI Act in principle prohibits real-time identification by law enforcement in publicly accessible spaces, while post-event identification is classed as high-risk and becomes subject to authorisation and other rules from December 2027.

CCTV at Hong Kong malls and the police SmartView programme

Hong Kong's Secretary for Security said in an interview on 18 October that some CCTV cameras at private shopping malls will be connected to the police SmartView programme, with talks in their final stage. He also said the public may not need to be told separately, and in February said that third-party connections are one-way and that footage on the government system is generally kept for 31 days. People travelling to Hong Kong for leisure or business may be caught on camera in public areas of malls, and places outside Hong Kong are not affected. According to Hong Kong media reports, facial recognition is being piloted in parts of the system.

Based on 23 sources, including Security Bureau, Hong Kong Police Force, Hong Kong Police Force, Legislative Council Secretariat

Tracking & Personal Data

AI training and cookie rules in the EU Digital Omnibus

On 21 October, EU member states agreed the Council of the EU's position on the draft Digital Omnibus, under which the GDPR would state that personal data may be processed on the basis of "legitimate interest" to develop and operate AI, and cookies would need no consent in more cases. It is the Council's position for negotiating with the European Parliament, which had not formed its own as of 23 October. The GDPR applies to personal data processed by businesses established in the EU, and also protects people in the EU who are offered services or whose behaviour is monitored by businesses outside it; otherwise, readers outside the EU are not directly affected.

Based on 24 sources, including Council of the European Union, EuropaWire, European Commission
